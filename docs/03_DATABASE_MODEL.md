# 03 — Conceptual Database Model (Provisional)

Status: **Conceptual, with the Identity & Organization group's Roles/Permissions, Departments/Teams/Positions, and Staff (Phases 5–7), the Clients group (Phase 8), the Staff Operations group (Phase 9), the Work Management group's Projects/Project Membership/Tasks/Work Logs (Phases 10–12), the HR group's Leave Management (Phase 13), the Communication group's Announcements, Notifications, and Messaging (Phases 14–16), the Scheduling group's Schedule Entries and Project Milestones (Phase 17), and the Operations group's Service Reports and Incident Reports plus the shared cross-cutting `attachments` table (Phases 18–19) now implemented.** This document identifies likely entities, relationships, ownership concepts, and areas needing later design decisions. It intentionally does not specify every column — that happens per-phase, when each area is actually implemented.

## 1. Entity Groups

### Identity & Organization
`users`, `staff`, `departments`, `teams`, `positions`, `roles`, `permissions`

- `users` — authentication identity (login, credentials, account state). Kept separate from `staff` so the concept of "a login" isn't inherently the same as "an HR staff record" (future non-staff users, or staff without app access). **Implemented as of Phase 4:** beyond Laravel's framework-default columns, `users` carries `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017) and `status` (`active`/`suspended`/`inactive`, `App\Enums\AccountStatus`). `personal_access_tokens` (Sanctum, DEC-022) stores mobile API tokens, polymorphically linked to `users` via `tokenable`. **Implemented as of Phase 5:** the transitional `is_admin` boolean (DEC-024) is retired/dropped; `users` now carries a nullable `role_id` FK instead (see below). **Implemented as of Phase 7 (DEC-030):** `users` gains an inverse `staff` relation (`User::staff(): HasOne`) — optional in both directions, not every User has a Staff record (e.g. the seeded local Administrator) and not every Staff record has a User.
- `staff` — the HR/organizational record: name, position, department/team, employment status, manager relationship. **Implemented as of Phase 7 (DEC-030):** `employee_number` (unique, admin-supplied), `first_name`/`last_name`/`preferred_name`, `company_email`/`company_phone`, `status` (`App\Enums\StaffStatus` — `active`/`inactive`/`separated`, an employment lifecycle distinct from `AccountStatus` and the future Phase 9 operational status), `hire_date`/`separation_date`, nullable `department_id`/`team_id`/`position_id` (Phase 6 references), a nullable self-referencing `manager_id`, and a nullable **unique** `user_id`.
- `departments`, `teams`, `positions` — organizational structure. **Implemented as of Phase 6 (DEC-029):** flat `departments` (no sub-department hierarchy); `teams` and `positions` each belong to **at most one** department via a nullable `department_id` FK — teams do not span multiple departments. **Implemented as of Phase 7:** `staff` now belongs to a `department`/`team` and holds a `position`, with Team/Department consistency validated at the application layer.
- `roles`, `permissions` — authorization. **Implemented as of Phase 5 (DEC-028):** many-to-many `roles`↔`permissions` (via `role_permissions`), but **one role per user** — `users.role_id`, a nullable FK, not a many-to-many `users`↔`roles` table. This is a deliberate resolution, not a placeholder: V1's actual requirement is one role per employee (CLAUDE.md's Phase 5 instructions); many-to-many user↔role infrastructure is explicitly not built without a demonstrated need. See `05_SECURITY_MODEL.md` and DEC-028.

### Clients
`clients`, `contacts`

- `contacts` belong to a `client` (many contacts per client). A `contact` is not a `user` — clients are external parties, not system logins, in V1. **Implemented as of Phase 8 (DEC-031):** `clients` — `public_id` (ULID), `client_code` (nullable, unique when present, admin-supplied), `name`, `status` (`App\Enums\ClientStatus` — `active`/`inactive`), `email`/`phone`/`website`, a small structured inline address (`address_line1`/`address_line2`/`city`/`state_province`/`postal_code`/`country`), `notes`. `contacts` — `public_id` (ULID), **required** `client_id` (`restrictOnDelete()` — never nullable, no client-less contacts, no many-to-many Contact↔Client relationship), `first_name`/`last_name`, `job_title`, `email`/`phone`, `is_primary` (boolean, at most one per client, application-enforced), `status` (`App\Enums\ContactStatus` — `active`/`inactive`), `notes`.

### Staff Operations
`staff_statuses`, `staff_checkins`

- `staff_statuses` — historical log of operational-status changes per staff member (DEC-010: append, don't overwrite). **Implemented as of Phase 9 (DEC-032):** `staff_id`, `status` (`App\Enums\OperationalStatus` — `available`/`busy`/`in_meeting`/`in_field`/`off_duty`, deliberately distinct from `staff.status`'s employment lifecycle), `changed_by_user_id` (nullable). "Current status" is a derived read (`Staff::latestOperationalStatus()`, `latestOfMany()`) — no denormalized cache column, avoiding dual-write risk.
- `staff_checkins` — explicit check-in events (DEC-005: not continuous tracking). **Implemented as of Phase 9 (DEC-032):** `public_id` (ULID), `staff_id`, required `latitude`/`longitude` (`decimal(10,7)`), optional `accuracy_meters`/`location_label` (free text — no separate `locations` catalog table was built; not warranted for V1)/`note`/`status` (an informational snapshot). "Current location" is likewise a derived read (`Staff::latestCheckIn()`, `latestOfMany()`). No `client_id` reference was added — check-ins are not yet connected to Clients/Projects (a future phase's concern if ever needed).

### Work Management
`projects`, `project_memberships`, `project_milestones`, `tasks`, `task_assignees`, `task_comments`, `work_logs`

- `projects` optionally belong to a `client`. **Implemented as of Phase 10 (DEC-033):** `public_id` (ULID), `project_code` (nullable, unique when present), `name`, `description`, `client_id` (nullable, `restrictOnDelete()`), `status` (`App\Enums\ProjectStatus` — `planned`/`active`/`on_hold`/`completed`/`cancelled`), `start_date`/`target_end_date`/`completed_date`, `notes`. No `project_manager_staff_id` — leadership lives entirely on Project Membership's role.
- `project_memberships` is the join between `projects` and `staff`, carrying a role-on-project (`App\Enums\ProjectMembershipRole` — `project_lead`/`member`). **Implemented as of Phase 10:** `project_id`/`staff_id` (both `restrictOnDelete()`), `role`, unique on `(project_id, staff_id)`. Represents the current roster only — no historical-period tracking, no `public_id` (addressed via its Project's/Staff's `public_id`).
- `project_milestones` belong to a `project`. **Not yet implemented** — deferred past Phase 10 (no milestone concept was introduced; see docs/phases/V1_PHASE_10_DEFINITION.md's explicit exclusions).
- `tasks` optionally belong to a `project` (DEC-006 — must support project-less tasks). **Implemented as of Phase 11 (DEC-034):** `public_id` (ULID), `project_id` (nullable, `restrictOnDelete()`), `title`, `description`, `status` (`App\Enums\TaskStatus`), `priority` (`App\Enums\TaskPriority`), `assignee_staff_id` (nullable, `restrictOnDelete()` — a single nullable assignee, not a `task_assignees` many-to-many pivot; no demonstrated V1 requirement for multiple simultaneous assignees), `created_by_user_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()`, accountability only), `due_date`, `completed_at` (server-controlled). `task_comments` was **not** built in Phase 11 — deferred to a future collaboration phase (see `docs/ROADMAP.md`'s Phase 11 note and DEC-034).
- `work_logs` belong to `staff` and optionally to a `task` and/or `project` (never neither) — historical activity entries, not payroll/attendance/billing. **Implemented as of Phase 12 (DEC-035):** `public_id` (ULID), `staff_id` (required, `restrictOnDelete()` — the performer), `task_id`/`project_id` (both nullable, `restrictOnDelete()` — at least one required, application-enforced; `project_id` is server-derived from `task_id` when set, never independently client-supplied alongside it, making Task↔Project inconsistency structurally impossible rather than merely validated), `created_by_user_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()`, accountability only — distinct from the performer), `work_date` (date, not future), `duration_minutes` (unsigned int, positive, capped at 1440), `description` (required, ≤2000 chars). No status/approval workflow, no start/end timestamps, no timer.

### HR
`leave_types`, `leave_requests`, `leave_request_actions`, `leave_balances`

- `leave_types` — configurable master data. **Implemented as of Phase 13 (DEC-036):** `public_id` (ULID), `name`, `code` (nullable, unique when present), `description`, `is_paid` (boolean, default `true`), `status` (`App\Enums\LeaveTypeStatus` — `active`/`inactive`), `sort_order`. No default annual allocation column — allocation is per Staff member (see `leave_balances`), and no country-specific statutory rules.
- `leave_requests` belong to `staff` and reference a `leave_type`. **Implemented as of Phase 13:** `public_id` (ULID), `staff_id`/`leave_type_id` (both `restrictOnDelete()`), `start_date`/`end_date`, server-computed `total_days` (inclusive calendar-day count, no business-day/holiday-aware calculation), required `reason`, `status` (`App\Enums\LeaveRequestStatus` — `pending`/`approved`/`rejected`/`cancelled`, no `draft`), `created_by_user_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()` — accountability, distinguishes self-service from Administrator-entered). Deliberately **no** `submitted_at`/`approved_at`/`rejected_at`/`cancelled_at` columns — submission is `created_at`, and every decision/cancellation timestamp lives only on `leave_request_actions` instead, avoiding a second source of truth for the same fact.
- `leave_request_actions` — historical decision record per request (DEC-010), resolving the open question below in favor of **single-stage** approval for V1: a `pending` request's current direct Manager or Administrator decides it, no multi-step chain. **Implemented as of Phase 13:** `leave_request_id` (`cascadeOnDelete()`), `action` (`App\Enums\LeaveRequestActionType` — `submitted`/`approved`/`rejected`/`cancelled`), `acted_by_user_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()`), `note`. Append-only; no `public_id` (never independently addressed by URL, read only via its parent Leave Request's own resource shape).
- `leave_balances` — per staff, per leave type, per period (calendar year, resolving `01_PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md` §6's accrual/period open question for V1: **no accrual engine** — `allocated_days` is an Administrator-set entitlement, and `used_days`/`pending_days`/`remaining_days` are **always derived live** from `leave_requests`, never a stored/cached column, extending DEC-032's "derive, don't cache" precedent). **Implemented as of Phase 13:** `staff_id`/`leave_type_id` (both `restrictOnDelete()`), `year`, `allocated_days`, `notes`, `created_by_user_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()`). Unique on `(staff_id, leave_type_id, year)`; no `public_id` (addressed only via its Staff member's `public_id` plus `leave_type_id`/`year` in an upsert-style request body).

### Scheduling
`schedule_entries`, `schedule_entry_participants`, `project_milestones`

- The former hypothetical unifying table (`calendar_events`) is **resolved (Phase 17, DEC-040): composed from source tables via a read-time API projection, never a stored/unified table.** `GET /api/v1/schedule` aggregates exactly four sources at read time — manually created `schedule_entries`, Task due dates (`tasks.due_date`), approved `leave_requests`, and `project_milestones` — tagged with a closed `App\Enums\ScheduleSourceType` discriminator (`schedule_entry`/`task`/`leave`/`project_milestone`). No calendar rows are copied into a generic table for the three aggregated sources; each remains its own system of record.
- `schedule_entries` — **Implemented as of Phase 17 (DEC-040):** `public_id` (ULID), `creator_staff_id` (required, `restrictOnDelete()` — a real business-authority column, not accountability metadata), `title`, `description` (nullable, ≤5,000 chars), `activity_type` (`App\Enums\ScheduleEntryActivityType` — `meeting`/`client_visit`/`service_appointment`/`company_event`/`training`/`internal_activity`/`other`, all values of one generic entity, never dedicated tables per activity kind), `starts_at`/`ends_at` (UTC `datetime`, a single pair used for both timed and all-day entries), `is_all_day` (boolean), `project_id` (nullable, `restrictOnDelete()`).
- `schedule_entry_participants` — a plain composite-PK pivot (`schedule_entry_id` `cascadeOnDelete()`, `staff_id` `restrictOnDelete()`) with **no RSVP/invited/accepted/declined/tentative state** — participation means only "appears on this Staff member's schedule and they may view it." The creator is never duplicated into this pivot.
- `project_milestones` — **Implemented as of Phase 17 (DEC-040), resolving the false "Phase 10 (Milestones)" roadmap dependency** (Phase 10 explicitly deferred this — see its own definition and this document's prior "not yet implemented" note): `public_id` (ULID), `project_id` (**required**, `restrictOnDelete()`), `title`, `due_date`, `status` (`App\Enums\ProjectMilestoneStatus` — `pending`/`completed`/`cancelled`, deliberately smaller than `TaskStatus` since a milestone is a point-in-time target, not ongoing work with its own progress states). No percent-complete, dependency graph, nested milestones, recurrence, or workflow engine.

### Communication
`conversations`, `conversation_members`, `messages`, `announcements`, `announcement_departments`, `announcement_teams`, `announcement_acknowledgements`, `notifications`

- `conversations` — direct, group, or project-linked. **Implemented as of Phase 16 (DEC-039):** `public_id` (ULID), `type` (`App\Enums\ConversationType` — `direct`/`group`/`project`, never client-supplied), `name` (nullable, group-only), `project_id` (nullable, **unique**, `restrictOnDelete()` — at most one per Project, lazily created on first use, never eagerly), `owner_staff_id` (nullable, `restrictOnDelete()`, group-only — the single permanent owner). `conversation_members` — resolved as a join to **`staff`, not `users`** (the originally-sketched "staff/users" ambiguity from this document's earlier draft): `conversation_id` (`cascadeOnDelete()`), `staff_id` (`restrictOnDelete()`), `last_read_message_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()` against `messages` — the sole read-position representation, no separate timestamp; unread count is always derived live). Direct conversations are canonical per unique Staff pair (found-or-created, exactly two fixed members); group conversations have owner-controlled membership (add/remove) plus member self-leave, with an owner-cannot-leave-while-others-remain invariant; project conversation membership derives exclusively from `project_memberships` and is never independently managed.
- `messages` belong to a `conversation` and an author (`staff`, not `users`). **Implemented as of Phase 16:** `public_id` (ULID), `conversation_id` (`cascadeOnDelete()`), `sender_staff_id` (`restrictOnDelete()`), `body` (plain text, ≤4,000 chars). Fully immutable after sending — no edit, no soft/hard delete — retained indefinitely.
- `announcements` are broadcast content. **Implemented as of Phase 14 (DEC-037):** `public_id` (ULID), `title`, `body` (plain text), `status` (`App\Enums\AnnouncementStatus` — `draft`/`published`/`archived`), `audience_type` (`App\Enums\AnnouncementAudienceType` — `company_wide`/`scoped`), `published_at`/`created_by_user_id`/`published_by_user_id`. The originally-sketched single `announcement_recipients` table is resolved as two dedicated many-to-many pivots, `announcement_departments`/`announcement_teams` (union semantics, no arbitrary individual-Staff/role targeting), rather than a polymorphic recipients table — no third target type was in view. `announcement_acknowledgements` records who has acknowledged it (`staff_id`, `created_at` as the acknowledgement timestamp) — an explicit, self-initiated, idempotent record, deliberately not "who has read it" (no read/unread tracking exists).
- `notifications` — system-generated, in-app, user-facing alerts. **Implemented as of Phase 15 (DEC-038):** `public_id` (ULID), `recipient_user_id` (`users`, `cascadeOnDelete()` — recipient identity is the `User` account, not `Staff`; see DEC-030's optional Staff↔User linkage), `type` (`App\Enums\NotificationType` — one case, `announcement_published`), `title`, `message` (short plain text), `source_type`/`source_public_id` (`App\Enums\NotificationSourceType` — one case, `announcement` — a **typed, non-polymorphic** source reference; the originally-sketched "likely polymorphic... Laravel's built-in notifications table shape" direction was resolved against, matching this codebase's established preference for a dedicated shape over generic polymorphism, DEC-017), `read_at` (nullable, set once, never reset). Created only by internal application code — currently only `AnnouncementController::publish()`'s audience fan-out (a publish-time snapshot, not a live query) — never by a client request; no Admin CRUD exists for this table.

### Operations
`service_reports`, `service_report_participants`, `service_report_actions`, `incidents`, `incident_actions`

- `service_reports` reference a required `client` and the `staff` who performed the work (`creator_staff_id`), optionally a `project`/`task`. **Implemented as of Phase 18 (DEC-041):** `public_id` (ULID), required `client_id` (`restrictOnDelete()` — the business anchor, never a generic internal activity record), nullable `project_id`/`task_id` (both `restrictOnDelete()`, mirroring DEC-006's optional-Project precedent — relational coherence between Client/Project/Task enforced at the application layer, not a DB `CHECK`), required `creator_staff_id` (`restrictOnDelete()` — a real business-authority column, not accountability metadata), `created_by_user_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()`, accountability only), `service_date` (date), `work_performed`/`findings`/`recommendations`/`follow_up_actions` (text, bounded at the validation layer), `site_representative_name` (nullable, plain text — no signature), `status` (`App\Enums\ServiceReportStatus` — `draft`/`submitted`/`reviewed`/`rejected`). `service_report_participants` — a plain pivot (no role/status hierarchy, no RSVP), mirroring `schedule_entry_participants` exactly; the creator is never duplicated into it. `service_report_actions` — append-only workflow-transition history (DEC-010), mirroring `leave_request_actions`' exact shape; no `public_id` (never independently addressed by URL).
- The originally-sketched `service_report_attachments` table is **resolved (Phase 18, DEC-041) as the shared `attachments` table** below, not a dedicated per-module table — see the Cross-Cutting section.
- `incident_reports` reference a reporting `staff` member, optionally a `client`/`project`/`task`, and optionally an assigned investigator `staff` member. **Implemented as of Phase 19 (DEC-042)** — this corrects `03_DATABASE_MODEL.md`'s original Phase-0-era sketch, which used the working names `incidents`/`incident_actions`; the canonical names, matching the product terminology and Service Reports' own convention, are `incident_reports`/`incident_report_actions`/`incident_report_participants` (`App\Models\IncidentReport`/`IncidentReportAction`). `public_id` (ULID), all three of `client_id`/`project_id`/`task_id` **nullable** (`restrictOnDelete()` — deliberately more permissive than Service Reports' required-Client anchor: an Incident Report may be entirely internal), required `reporter_staff_id` (`restrictOnDelete()` — immutable after creation), nullable `assigned_to_staff_id` (`restrictOnDelete()` — a single investigator, mutated only via dedicated assign/reassign/start-investigation action endpoints, never a generic update), `created_by_user_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()`, accountability only), `occurred_at` (a genuine UTC `datetime` — deliberately unlike `service_reports.service_date`'s date-only column, since an incident has a real moment of occurrence; no separate `reported_at`, since `created_at` already serves that purpose), `location` (nullable free text — no `locations` master-data table, no GPS/coordinates, mirroring `staff_checkins.location_label`'s Phase 9 precedent), `incident_type` (`App\Enums\IncidentReportType`: `workplace_safety`/`client_site`/`operational`/`property_equipment_damage`/`it_security`/`other` — one generic entity, never dedicated tables per category), `severity` (`App\Enums\IncidentSeverity`: `low`/`medium`/`high`/`critical`, default `medium`), narrative fields (`description` required; `immediate_action_taken`/`root_cause`/`corrective_action`/`preventive_action`/`follow_up_actions`/`resolution`/`people_involved`/`witness_notes` all nullable — the latter two are narrative stand-ins for external/customer persons and witnesses, deliberately never structured tables or linked to `users`/`contacts`), `status` (`App\Enums\IncidentReportStatus`: `reported`/`under_investigation`/`resolved`/`closed` — an investigation-oriented lifecycle, deliberately not Service Reports' draft/submitted/reviewed/rejected shape; `reopen` is an action, never a fifth persisted status). `incident_report_participants` — a plain pivot (no role/status hierarchy, no RSVP, no witness/injury-role enum), mirroring `service_report_participants` exactly; the reporter/assignee are never duplicated into it. `incident_report_actions` — append-only history (DEC-010) mirroring `service_report_actions`' exact shape (`App\Enums\IncidentReportActionType`: `reported`/`assigned`/`reassigned`/`investigation_started`/`resolved`/`closed`/`reopened`); unlike Service Reports, the initial `reported` action is recorded by the model's own `created` event, not the controller, so every creation path produces a complete history.

### Cross-Cutting
`attachments`, `audit_logs`, `settings`

- `notifications` was originally sketched here as a cross-cutting table alongside Communication — now implemented (Phase 15, DEC-038) as a single table under Communication above; no separate cross-cutting copy exists.
- `attachments` — **resolved (Phase 18, DEC-041; extended Phase 19, DEC-042):** a single shared table, built in Phase 18 because Service Reports required it and Phase 19 (Incident Reports) was the concrete next consumer — exactly as anticipated. **Not a generic polymorphic table** (never a Laravel-style `attachable_type` raw-PHP-class-name column paired with a bare `attachable_id` carrying no real foreign key) — instead a typed, non-polymorphic discriminator (`owner_type`, `App\Enums\AttachmentOwnerType`: `service_report`/`incident_report`) paired with a genuine, real, `cascadeOnDelete()` foreign key **per owner type** (`service_report_id`/`incident_report_id`, each nullable — every row populates exactly one). This preserves full DB-level referential integrity while remaining trivially extensible — Phase 19's own extension (a purely additive migration: a new nullable `incident_report_id` column/FK, a new enum case, and widening `service_report_id` from `NOT NULL` to nullable since a second owner type now exists) is itself the proof; no existing Service Report attachment row or behavior changed. Columns: `public_id`, `owner_type`, `service_report_id`, `incident_report_id`, `original_filename` (display only, never the physical path), `storage_disk`, `storage_path` (a generated ULID-based name), `mime_type`, `size_bytes`, `uploaded_by_user_id` (nullable, `nullOnDelete()`). See `App\Support\Attachments\AttachmentDisk`/`App\Services\Attachments\AttachmentStorage` (the latter's `store()` gained an optional directory-prefix parameter, defaulting to `'service-reports'` so Phase 18 behavior is unaffected; Incident Reports passes `'incident-reports'`).
- `audit_logs` — cross-cutting record of significant actions: actor, action, subject type/id, before/after or metadata, timestamp. Introduced early (Core Architecture / Authentication phase) as shared infrastructure so later modules use it rather than invent their own.
- `settings` — application-level configuration that must be admin-editable at runtime (as opposed to `.env`/config files, which are deploy-time).

## 2. Ownership & Ambient Concepts

- **`staff` is the organizational anchor.** Most operational records (statuses, check-ins, work logs, leave, task assignments) hang off `staff`, not `users` directly, keeping "who is this person in the org" separate from "how do they log in."
- **`client` is a secondary anchor** for external-facing work (projects, service reports, some incidents).
- **History over mutation** (DEC-010) applies specifically to: staff status, leave approval decisions, incident progress. Other entities may use simple status columns where no meaningful history is needed (e.g. a task's `completed_at` timestamp) — this is a per-entity judgment call at implementation time, not a blanket rule.
- **Audit logging** (DEC-009) is expected to wrap administrative/workflow-significant mutations broadly, likely via a model observer/trait pattern established once and reused, rather than hand-written per module.

## 3. Deliberately Undecided

- Exact column lists, types, and constraints — decided per-phase against real requirements, not guessed here.
- Soft-deletes vs hard-deletes per entity.
- ~~Whether `attachments` is one shared polymorphic table or per-module tables.~~ **Resolved (Phase 18, DEC-041):** one shared table, but typed/non-polymorphic (a real FK per owner type), never a generic `attachable_type`/`attachable_id` pair — see §1 above.
- ~~Whether `calendar_events` is unified or composed from source tables.~~ **Resolved (Phase 17, DEC-040):** composed from source tables via a read-time API projection — see §1 above.
- ~~Multi-step vs single-step leave approval.~~ **Resolved (Phase 13, DEC-036):** single-stage — see §1 above.

**Resolved (Phase 3, DEC-017):** primary keys are numeric `BIGINT` (`$table->id()`); externally addressable entities additionally get a `ULID public_id` column, added when each entity is actually built. Likely remaining candidates: `clients`, `projects`, `tasks`, `leave_requests`, `service_reports`, `incidents` (all listed in §1 above) — decided per-entity, not applied blanket. Pivot/history tables (`project_members`, `staff_statuses`, `leave_approvals`, etc.) generally don't need one. **Applied in Phase 4** to `users` — the first entity to actually carry one. **Applied in Phase 6** to `departments`/`teams`/`positions` (admin-manageable organization structure, unlike the fixed internal `roles`/`permissions` catalog, which deliberately does not carry one — see below). **Applied in Phase 7** to `staff`.

**Resolved (Phase 4):** `users` gained real authentication columns (`public_id`, `status`, `is_admin`) and `personal_access_tokens` (Sanctum) was added — see §1 above and DEC-022/DEC-024.

**Resolved (Phase 5):** `roles`, `permissions`, `role_permissions` were added; `users.is_admin` (DEC-024) was retired and replaced with `users.role_id` (nullable FK, one role per user) — see §1 above and DEC-028. `roles`/`permissions` deliberately do **not** carry a `public_id` — they're a small, fixed, internally-managed system catalog (not a user-facing externally addressable business entity in DEC-017's sense), so the numeric PK strategy alone is sufficient; `UserResource` exposes the role's *name*, never its internal ID, so nothing external needs to address a role by public identifier.

**Resolved (Phase 4A):** the production database direction (MySQL, DEC-016) is now also the standard local development database, via the Docker Compose `mysql` service (DEC-027) — all current migrations were verified to run cleanly against real MySQL 8.4, not just SQLite. SQLite remains the automated-test database (unaffected, isolated per `phpunit.xml`) and is still an option for a developer running the backend directly rather than via Docker.

**Resolved (Phase 6):** `departments`, `teams`, `positions` were added — see §1 above and DEC-029. Each carries a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — these are admin-manageable business entities, not an internal fixed catalog like `roles`/`permissions`). `teams`/`positions` carry a nullable `department_id` (`restrictOnDelete()`); all three share `App\Enums\OrganizationStatus` (`active`/`inactive`) rather than soft-deletes.

**Resolved (Phase 7):** `staff` was added — see §1 above and DEC-030. Deliberately separate from `users` (see "Ownership & Ambient Concepts" below); `staff.user_id` is nullable and unique, not a forced 1:1. `staff` carries its own three-state `App\Enums\StaffStatus` (`active`/`inactive`/`separated`) rather than reusing `AccountStatus` or `OrganizationStatus`. The Manager relationship is a self-referencing `manager_id`, guarded against self-reference and (bounded) reporting cycles at the application layer.

**Resolved (Phase 8):** `clients`/`contacts` were added — see §1 above and DEC-031. Each carries a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — admin-manageable business entities). `contacts.client_id` is required (never nullable) and `restrictOnDelete()` — a Contact always belongs to exactly one Client. `clients`/`contacts` each carry their own two-state lifecycle (`App\Enums\ClientStatus`/`ContactStatus`) rather than reusing `OrganizationStatus` (a distinct domain concept, even though the state shape is identical) or introducing `SoftDeletes`.

**Resolved (Phase 9):** `staff_statuses`/`staff_checkins` were added — see §1 above and DEC-032. Neither carries a `public_id` matching Department/Team/Position/Staff/Client/Contact's admin-manageable-entity pattern: `staff_checkins` does get one (an Administrator addresses a single check-in directly for deletion), but `staff_statuses` deliberately doesn't (a status entry is never independently addressed by URL — DEC-017's "pivot/history tables generally don't need one"). Both `cascadeOnDelete()` on their parent `staff` row (child data with no independent meaning), unlike every `restrictOnDelete()` relationship elsewhere in the schema (which protects master data other rows still depend on).

**Resolved (Phase 10):** `projects`/`project_memberships` were added — see §1 above and DEC-033. `projects` carries a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — an admin-manageable business entity); `project_memberships` deliberately doesn't (never independently addressed by URL — addressed via its Project's/Staff's `public_id`, same reasoning as `staff_statuses`, Phase 9). Both `project_memberships` foreign keys are `restrictOnDelete()` (master data future modules will anchor to), not `cascadeOnDelete()` (contrast Phase 9's Staff-owned child data).

**Resolved (Phase 11):** `tasks` was added — see §1 above and DEC-034. Carries a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — an admin/business-manageable entity). `project_id` is nullable (DEC-006 — an independent Task is a first-class case, not an edge case) and `restrictOnDelete()`; `assignee_staff_id` is likewise `restrictOnDelete()` — a Task with any assignment history blocks Staff deletion, regardless of the Task's own status. `created_by_user_id` is the first `nullOnDelete()` foreign key on a non-history table (mirroring `staff_statuses.changed_by_user_id`'s pattern, Phase 9, rather than Project/Client/Staff's `restrictOnDelete()` master-data pattern) — accountability metadata, not master data other rows depend on. The `task_assignees` many-to-many and `task_comments` tables sketched in §1 above were **not** built — resolved in favor of a single nullable assignee and deferred collaboration features respectively, both per the governing Phase 11 instructions.

**Resolved (Phase 12):** `work_logs` was added — see §1 above and DEC-035. Carries a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — independently addressable via `/api/v1/work-logs/{public_id}`/`/api/v1/me/work-logs/{public_id}`, unlike `project_memberships`/`staff_statuses`). `staff_id` (the performer) is required and `restrictOnDelete()` — unlike Task's nullable `assignee_staff_id`, a Work Log always has a performer. `task_id`/`project_id` are both nullable `restrictOnDelete()` foreign keys, but at least one is required (application-enforced, no DB `CHECK` constraint, consistent with this document's existing precedent of enforcing cross-field business rules at the application layer — e.g. Contact's single-primary-per-Client, Phase 8) — a Work Log referencing neither would be a generic, un-anchored activity log, explicitly out of scope. `project_id` is genuinely persisted (not purely derived at read time) because a Work Log may reference a Project directly with no specific Task; when a `task_id` is set, `project_id` is always server-derived from that Task's own `project_id` (itself possibly `null` for an independent Task) and never independently client-supplied — Task↔Project inconsistency is structurally impossible, not merely validated. `created_by_user_id` mirrors `tasks.created_by_user_id`'s `nullOnDelete()` accountability-only pattern exactly.

**Resolved (Phase 13):** `leave_types`/`leave_requests`/`leave_request_actions`/`leave_balances` were added — see §1 above and DEC-036. `leave_types`/`leave_requests` carry a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — independently addressable business entities); `leave_request_actions`/`leave_balances` deliberately don't (never independently addressed by URL, same reasoning as `staff_statuses`/`project_memberships`). `leave_requests.staff_id`/`leave_type_id` and `leave_balances.staff_id`/`leave_type_id` are all `restrictOnDelete()` (HR history/master data other rows depend on, not Staff-owned child data with no independent meaning); `leave_request_actions.leave_request_id` is `cascadeOnDelete()` (child data with no independent meaning apart from its Leave Request — in practice never fires, since a Leave Request is never itself deleted). Usage (`used_days`/`pending_days`/`remaining_days`) is never a column on `leave_balances` at all — always derived live from `leave_requests`, extending DEC-032's "derive, don't cache" precedent from operational status/location to balance consumption and (via `leave_requests`' own missing `submitted_at`/`approved_at`/etc. columns) decision timestamps as well.

**Resolved (Phase 14):** `announcements`/`announcement_departments`/`announcement_teams`/`announcement_acknowledgements` were added — see §1 above and DEC-037. `announcements` carries a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — independently addressable via `/api/v1/announcements/{public_id}`/`/api/v1/me/announcements/{public_id}`); the two audience pivots and the acknowledgement table deliberately don't (never independently addressed by URL, same reasoning as `role_permissions`/`project_memberships`/`staff_statuses`). `announcement_departments.department_id`/`announcement_teams.team_id` are `restrictOnDelete()` (an Announcement's historical audience is never silently orphaned by an org-structure change); `announcement_acknowledgements.staff_id` is likewise `restrictOnDelete()` (the same relational-integrity philosophy as every other Staff-referencing history table); all three tables' `announcement_id` is `cascadeOnDelete()` (child data with no independent meaning apart from its Announcement — in practice never fires, since only a still-`draft` Announcement can be hard-deleted, and a draft can have no audience/acknowledgement rows referenced by anyone yet). `conversations`/`messages`/`notifications` were added in Phases 15–16 — see below.

**Resolved (Phase 15):** `notifications` was added — see §1 above and DEC-038. Carries a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — independently addressable via `/api/v1/me/notifications/{public_id}`); `recipient_user_id` is the first Communication-group foreign key to reference `users` rather than `staff` — a deliberate, DEC-038-recorded exception to this document's own "`staff` is the organizational anchor" principle (§2), since a Notification can only ever be consumed through an authenticated login and Staff may exist without one.

**Resolved (Phase 16):** `conversations`/`conversation_members`/`messages` were added — see §1 above and DEC-039. `conversations`/`messages` each carry a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — independently addressable, `messages` despite having no individual show/edit/delete route, mirroring `notifications`' "listed/paginated resource" precedent rather than `leave_request_actions`' "sub-list only" one); `conversation_members` deliberately doesn't (never independently addressed by URL, same reasoning as `project_memberships`). Unlike Notifications, Messaging's participant identity reverts to **`staff`, not `users`** — DEC-039 explains why DEC-038's User-based reasoning doesn't generalize here. `conversations.project_id`/`owner_staff_id` and `messages.sender_staff_id` are `restrictOnDelete()` (anchor/historical data other rows or facts depend on); `conversation_members.conversation_id`/`messages.conversation_id` are `cascadeOnDelete()` (child data with no independent meaning apart from their conversation); `conversation_members.staff_id` is `restrictOnDelete()` (mirrors `project_memberships`' current-roster protection). `conversation_members.last_read_message_id` is nullable/`nullOnDelete()` against `messages` — the sole read-position representation (no separate timestamp column), extending DEC-032/DEC-036's "derive, don't cache" precedent to unread state.

**Resolved (Phase 17):** `schedule_entries`/`schedule_entry_participants`/`project_milestones` were added — see §1 above and DEC-040. `schedule_entries`/`project_milestones` each carry a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — independently addressable via `/api/v1/schedule-entries/{public_id}`/`/api/v1/projects/{project}/milestones/{public_id}`); `schedule_entry_participants` deliberately doesn't (never independently addressed by URL, same reasoning as `project_memberships`/`conversation_members`). `schedule_entries.creator_staff_id` and `project_milestones.project_id` are both `restrictOnDelete()` (business-authority/anchor data other rows or facts depend on, not child data with no independent meaning); `schedule_entry_participants.schedule_entry_id` is `cascadeOnDelete()` (child data with no independent meaning apart from its Schedule Entry), `staff_id` `restrictOnDelete()` (mirrors `conversation_members.staff_id`/`project_memberships.staff_id`). `schedule_entries.starts_at`/`ends_at` are the first genuine time-of-day (not date-only) columns in this codebase — see `02_ARCHITECTURE.md` §27's Date/Time design.

**Resolved (Phase 18):** `service_reports`/`service_report_participants`/`service_report_actions`/`attachments` were added — see §1 above and DEC-041. `service_reports`/`attachments` each carry a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — independently addressable via `/api/v1/service-reports/{public_id}`/`/api/v1/service-reports/{public_id}/attachments/{public_id}`); `service_report_participants`/`service_report_actions` deliberately don't (never independently addressed by URL, same reasoning as `schedule_entry_participants`/`leave_request_actions`). `service_reports.client_id`/`project_id`/`task_id`/`creator_staff_id` are all `restrictOnDelete()` (business-anchor/historical data other rows depend on); `service_report_participants.service_report_id`/`service_report_actions.service_report_id`/`attachments.service_report_id` are `cascadeOnDelete()` (child data with no independent meaning apart from their Service Report — in practice `service_report_actions`/`attachments` cascades only ever fire for a still-`draft` report, since only a draft may ever be deleted). This is the first phase to introduce genuine file storage (`attachments.storage_disk`/`storage_path`) — see `App\Support\Attachments\AttachmentDisk` and `02_ARCHITECTURE.md` §28.

**Resolved (Phase 19):** `incident_reports`/`incident_report_participants`/`incident_report_actions` were added, and `attachments` was extended — see §1 above and DEC-042. This corrects the Phase-0-era working names `incidents`/`incident_actions` this document originally sketched to the canonical `incident_reports`/`incident_report_actions` (matching product terminology and Service Reports' own naming). `incident_reports` carries a `public_id` (ULID, DEC-017 — independently addressable via `/api/v1/incident-reports/{public_id}`); `incident_report_participants`/`incident_report_actions` deliberately don't (never independently addressed by URL, same reasoning as `service_report_participants`/`service_report_actions`). `incident_reports.client_id`/`project_id`/`task_id`/`reporter_staff_id`/`assigned_to_staff_id` are all `restrictOnDelete()` (business-anchor/historical data other rows depend on) — unlike `service_reports`, `client_id`/`project_id`/`task_id` are **all nullable** (an Incident Report may be entirely internal, a deliberate divergence from Service Reports' required-Client anchor, DEC-042). `incident_report_participants.incident_report_id`/`incident_report_actions.incident_report_id`/`attachments.incident_report_id` are `cascadeOnDelete()` (child data with no independent meaning apart from their Incident Report) — unlike Service Reports, `incident_report_actions`' cascade fires routinely rather than rarely, since the model's own `created` event logs a `reported` action for every Incident Report at the moment it's created, so even the earliest deletable (reported, unassigned) incident already carries at least one action row. `attachments` gains its second owner type (`AttachmentOwnerType::IncidentReport`, a new nullable `incident_report_id` FK) via a purely additive migration; `attachments.service_report_id` is widened from `NOT NULL` to nullable in the same migration (a necessary consequence of a second owner type existing — every row now populates exactly one of the two FKs), with no change to any existing Service Report attachment row.

This document should be revisited and updated (not silently replaced) each time a phase implements one of these areas for real, so it stays a useful map rather than going stale.
